◇ 03 · Evidence

Where the evidence came from

I was the only designer on CPQ, so I ran the research myself. I held interviews and contextual inquiry sessions with brokers and operations staff. I reviewed six months of product usage analytics. I ran seven stakeholder workshops with the product owner and sales team, and we agreed on how success would be measured before I designed any screens.

That is a mix of broker and ops staff observation, usage data, and stakeholder input. I am not claiming a large primary research study.

synthesis from Zywave's cross-product navigation discovery. CPQ was included, but this board is not CPQ-only research
◇ 04 · What I tried

What I tried before it worked

The plan I started with was to redesign the pricing screen. Everyone on the team believed it was the slow step, and I did too.

Contextual inquiry did not support it. Brokers had built outside spreadsheets because the tool could not duplicate a quote, and weak validation was making errors expensive. Those two problems cost more than the screen we had blamed.

I dropped the pricing-screen plan and rebuilt the work around quote duplication and inline validation. That is what shipped.

◇ 05 · Constraints

Constraints and what we did

CONSTRAINT
WHAT WE DID

The product already had a design system

I worked inside it and contributed reusable product patterns.

Long-time users had built habits

We kept core workflows recognizable and changed the patterns around them.

Brokers do not only work at desks

Quote review and editing work across desktop, tablet, and mobile.

Technical limits showed up late

I brought engineering into the design phase mid-project. A few screens had already needed rework.

◇ 06 · System and close

Where the design system took over

CPQ already had a design system, and I did not build it. Duplication, status, and validation were designed with its components, so they look and behave like the rest of the product. I added reusable patterns where it had gaps.

If I did this again, I would involve engineering from the first week and plan a phased rollout. We launched everything at once, and a staged release would have let us learn from real use.

Research kept me from polishing the most visible screen while the costlier workaround stayed in place.
