◇ 00 · Cover
CASE STUDY 02 · FISERV × DESJARDINS PHASE 1 DELIVERED

I rebuilt a card admin console around four roles and delivered Phase 1 early

360 Control is the console banks use to run commercial card programs. I redesigned it for Desjardins against a fixed conversion deadline, and I kept AI out of the product on purpose.

THE PROBLEM The old issuer console followed the backend, with no shared patterns and weak mobile support. Then Desjardins signed with bilingual, branded and mobile requirements and a fixed conversion date.
WHAT I DID I rebuilt it around four roles and seven areas named for the work, with one grid model, shared confirmation patterns for risky actions and a Business Audit Log. I kept AI out on purpose.
HOW WE GOT THERE With no direct access to admins, I ran a heuristic review, sessions with internal experts, requirements and support tickets. Then I checked the designs in weekly reviews and monthly CAT drops.
THE OUTCOME Phase 1 was delivered by April 2026, ahead of the June 2026 deadline. I have no measured task-time or support-volume numbers, so I make no claim about them.

◇ 01 · Old to New

From a console built for the system to one built for the work

The old console carried years of incremental development. It had no shared patterns, navigation that followed the backend, and limited mobile responsiveness. Desjardins then signed, with bilingual Canadian French, a branded experience, mobile access, and a June 2026 conversion date.

THE OLD PATH
  1. Open a module named after how the system was built.
  2. Dig through grids and deep menus for a critical action, such as a credit limit change or a card replacement.
  3. Make the change with inconsistent preview and confirmation.
  4. Jump to other modules to finish the same task.
  5. Raise a support request when a compliance officer needed to know who changed a limit.
THE NEW PATH
  1. Land on an overview that shows which issuer you are managing and what needs attention.
  2. Pick one of seven areas, named after the work you came to do.
  3. Find the action where it lives, in a Users and Cards hub with search, filters, and contextual actions.
  4. Confirm it with the same confirmation patterns used everywhere else.
  5. Open the Business Audit Log in the product, then filter by date, event type, and user, and export to CSV.

Two decisions carried most of this. I split setup work from day-to-day work in the structure, so admins know which kind of task they are in before they open a feature. And each of the four roles sees only what that role may do, with account emulation so an admin can check what another user sees.

◇ 02 · Evidence

Where the evidence came from

I did not have direct access to issuer administrators. Client access was limited and the timeline was tight. So I used what I could get. I ran a heuristic evaluation of the legacy platform, held structured sessions with internal subject-matter experts and VP-level stakeholders, read the business requirement documents, and analyzed support tickets.

Validation came from weekly stakeholder reviews, refinement sessions with engineering (5 to 7 stories a session, with 10 engineers), and monthly CAT drops from June 2025 with realistic data and Desjardins participants. That feedback changed the Advanced Spend Controls labels, the navigation, contact-details behavior, and how easy it was to find Customize Columns.

This is stakeholder evidence, not observation of admins at work. Next time I would watch a few real issuer admins earlier.

◇ 03 · What I Tried

What I tried and cut before it worked

A full report builder

A dynamic report builder did not fit the timeline. I narrowed it to strong filtering and Excel or CSV export. On-screen charts stayed on the roadmap.

The full program hierarchy

The richer interaction for program hierarchy could not fit the contract date. I scoped a coherent Phase 1 and kept the fuller model for Phase 2, rather than ship half of it.

Two password flows

First-time access could use a system-generated password or one the user sets. I designed both and A/B tested them. The user-created flow removed the email-retrieval and copy steps, and it went into the Desjardins production build. I have no conversion uplift number to share.

◇ 05 · AI and System
A DELIBERATE DESIGN DECISION

Where AI stops, and the design system takes over

There is no AI inside 360 Control. Administrators define authorization strategies, spend controls, and permissions, and the product applies them the same way every time. The screens show the rule, its scope, and what changed. I made the opposite call on dispute intake, where AI suggests and a person reviews. That is in Disputes Workspace and AI Dispute Intake.

I did use AI to design it. I built interactive prototypes in VS Code with Claude Code, and subject-matter experts clicked through them before development. I mentored interns by writing prompts and design instructions, then reviewing their AI-generated screens with them. I also proposed a design pull-request workflow, which has not been adopted.

The screens sit on Fiserv's shared design foundation, which I extended with data-dense grids, form conventions, and confirmation patterns. One grid model runs across Users, Cards, Transactions, and Audit Logs, so you learn it once. The foundation is covered in the design system study.

In a product where one wrong click moves real money, a clear structure is part of the safety.
