◇ 01 · From manual to checked

From typing from memory to checking a suggestion

Every dispute starts with intake. An agent listens to a cardholder and decides what kind of dispute it is. Agents carried roughly 30 to 50 active cases under FCBA and Reg E deadlines, so intake happened fast, with a customer waiting.

BEFORE · MANUAL INTAKE
  1. The agent listens to the cardholder.
  2. The agent picks the dispute type from memory.
  3. The agent types each detail by hand.
  4. The reason for the choice stays in the agent's head or a free-text note.
AFTER · SUGGEST, THEN CHECK
  1. The agent describes the dispute in plain language.
  2. The model pre-fills 6 to 10 attributes, each tagged High, Medium, or Low.
  3. The agent confirms, edits, or overrides each field.
  4. The log keeps the suggestion, the confidence, the action, and the final value.
◇ 02 · The evidence

Where the evidence came from

For much of this work I could not reach agents directly. I learned from product managers, operations leaders, compliance partners, and internal SMEs, through interviews and walkthroughs. I checked what I heard against requirements reviews and QA feedback.

So the reasoning behind this pilot comes from stakeholders and SMEs, not from a study of agents using it. I did not have access to the formal evaluation rubric, and I did not train or tune the model. This page shows the design and the signals it can produce. It does not report model performance.

◇ 03 · What I tried

What I tried before it worked

Automate intake outright. The model would classify the dispute and fill the fields. I dropped it. An agent cannot defend a value they cannot inspect, and the regulator holds the agent responsible, not the model.

Show a confidence percentage. An early version of the pilot showed numbers. Stakeholders told me a percentage did not tell an agent what to do next.

What I settled on. Each field shows High, Medium, or Low, paired with a next step. High means review and accept. Medium means verify before continuing. Low means manual review is recommended. This was a decision about how to communicate uncertainty. I have no evidence about how well the model was calibrated.

◇ 05 · Where the agent overrules

Where a person overrules the model

The agent can accept a suggestion, correct it, or override it. An override needs a documented reason, and the final value is logged next to what the AI suggested. The agent stays accountable for what gets submitted.

Because the pilot was new, there was no earlier baseline. I designed the log to serve compliance first and to show the team where the model gets corrected. The signals it can produce are acceptance rate, override rate by confidence level, and the fields overridden most often. These are signals the design supports, not results I measured.

◇ 06 · Constraints

Constraints

CONSTRAINTWHAT WE DID
FCBA and Reg E deadlines, with 30 to 50 active cases per agentA confidence cue and a next step on every field, so an agent can scan instead of reread.
The agent is accountable in a regulated workflowThe AI assists classification and intake only. The agent confirms every field, and the AI never approves, denies, or finalizes a dispute.
Limited direct access to agentsInterviews and walkthroughs with SMEs and stakeholders, checked against requirements reviews and QA feedback.
A new feature with no baseline, and no access to the evaluation rubricAn audit log that records the suggestion, the confidence, the human action, and the final value, so signals can be observed later.
This is a pilotThe prototype on this page is a simulated, client-side rebuild that uses no real data.
◇ 07 · Where AI Stops

Where AI helps, and where it stops

AI helps at one moment: reading a plain-language description and pre-filling the intake fields. It stops there. Everything after that is a person making a decision or a rule a person wrote.

The Virtual Analyst, the Match Index, and the advanced search in the workspace use no AI. Administrators define the rules, and the system runs them. I cover those in Disputes Workspace, and the shared components in the design system case study.

The AI classifies, the agent confirms, and the log keeps the record.
